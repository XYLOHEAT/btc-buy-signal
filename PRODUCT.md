# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Three groups share one screen:

- **The owner**, checking on a phone every week or two before deciding how much to DCA into Bitcoin. Knows the indices; wants the answer in one glance and the evidence one scroll away.
- **Friends the owner sends the link to.** They hold or follow BTC but can't read on-chain metrics. They need the verdict in plain words and a way to learn what each index means without leaving the page.
- **People who find it on their own.** No context at all. Before trusting anything they need to know what the score is, what it is not (a trade signal), and where the data comes from.

Job to be done: "Is now a good time to accumulate BTC, and how hard should I lean in?", answered from slow valuation data rather than price action.

## Product Purpose

A free, static, client-side dashboard that combines six long-horizon Bitcoin valuation indices (Ahr999, MVRV Z-Score, 200W MA Multiple, Pi Cycle Top, Mayer Multiple, Puell Multiple) into one 0–100 accumulation score, and shows its reasoning: per-index readings, a forward-return backtest by zone, a DCA simulator, cycle comparison and a monthly history heatmap. Thai and English.

Success: a visitor gets the verdict in a few seconds, can check why in under a minute, and leaves calmer rather than more excited. It paces DCA; it never tells anyone to trade (ADR-006). It stays non-commercial (Coin Metrics data is CC BY-NC).

## Brand Personality

**Professional · precise · understated.**

The voice of an analyst's note: state the reading, give the number, show the evidence, name the caveat. Calm, never hype. Numbers are exact and sourced; words are plain. Confidence comes from showing the data, not from styling.

Reference: the Financial Times, specifically the way it reads as analysis rather than a feed. Numbers always sit in context, charts are restrained but exact, typography and hierarchy carry the page, and one accent color does the pointing.

## Anti-references

- **AI slop**: anything that reads as generated by default, such as small uppercase eyebrows over every section, identical cards, decorative gradients, generic "modern" polish.
- **Dark crypto neon**: black plus aurora mesh, glassmorphism and glow. This project's first build looked like this and was scrapped (ADR-007).
- **FOMO trading apps**: flashing tickers, saturated red and green everywhere, "BUY NOW" urgency, countdowns.
- **SaaS dashboard templates**: grids of identical KPI cards, big-number-small-label hero metrics, gradient accents.
- **Cartoon and meme-coin aesthetics**: loud colors, mascots that upstage the data.

## Design Principles

1. **Verdict first, evidence on demand.** The score and its zone read in one glance; every claim is one scroll or one tap from its evidence (index values, backtest, sources).
2. **Calm is a feature.** The product exists to slow decisions down. Nothing pulses, flashes or urges; color signals state, not excitement.
3. **Show the method, name the limits.** Formulas, data sources, data delays and caveats are visible and honest. Trust comes from transparency, not polish.
4. **Precision over decoration.** Exact numbers in monospaced numerals, consistent units and rounding, charts without chartjunk. An element that doesn't help read the data goes.
5. **Legible to a newcomer, respectful of an expert.** A plain-language verdict and per-index explanations for first-timers; raw values and full history for people who read on-chain data.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- **WCAG 2.2 AA everywhere**: text contrast ≥ 4.5:1 in both light and dark themes, visible focus states, 44px touch targets, semantic landmarks.
- **AAA contrast on the primary reading path** (7:1, or 4.5:1 for large text): the score, the zone verdict, the live price, index values and body copy.
- Zone meaning never relies on color alone; every colored state also carries a text label, so it stays readable with color blindness.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` is respected; motion only conveys state and always has a static alternative.
- Full Thai and English parity. Thai needs its own line-height and font coverage, not Latin defaults.
